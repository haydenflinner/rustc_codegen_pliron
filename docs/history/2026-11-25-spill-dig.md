# 2026-11-25: executed spill/reload/stack-traffic excess vs LLVM — decomposition

Read-only diagnostic (no source edits; vendor/regalloc2 and pliron-clif-opt are
owned by other streams). Question: where does the executed stack-side and
move-side excess vs LLVM come from on `blake3-scalar`, `pulldown-cmark`,
`rust-compression`, `rust-html-rewriter`, grouped by mechanism, and what is
fixable. Fresh baseline: `harness/rastat.py dyn` at HEAD, saved
`target/rastat/dyn-spilldig.json` (classes: `st`/`ld` = stack store/load,
`csr` = callee-saved save/restore, `frame` = prologue/epilogue sp ops,
`mov` split by `move_kinds`: edge/callarg/callret/ret/inblock).

## 1. Per-program totals (executed, ours vs LLVM)

| program | insts gap | st | ld | st+ld | csr | frame | mov | mov:edge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| blake3-scalar | +1,818k | -160k | **+991k** | +831k | +154k | +224k | -628k | +20k |
| pulldown-cmark | +1,376k | +144k | -307k | -163k | +116k | +102k | +999k | **+572k** |
| rust-compression | +1,373k | +15k | -459k | -444k | -15k | +19k | +707k | **+941k** |
| rust-html-rewriter | +7,172k | +513k | +138k | +651k | +325k | +277k | +1,269k | **+1,120k** |

Two different problems, not one:

- **Real stack-mem excess** (st+ld) is concentrated in **blake3** (+831k,
  all in `compress_in_place` reloads) and **html** (+651k, dominated by
  explicit-slot *stores*). Compression is net **negative** (-444k mem ops)
  and pulldown roughly neutral (-163k): their overhead is not stack traffic.
- **`mov:edge`** (blockparam/phi copies on control-flow edges — runs of moves
  ending at a jump or starting at a branch target) is the single largest
  overhead class on 3 of 4 programs: html +1.12M (x3.9), compression +941k
  (x24), pulldown +572k (x2.5). blake3 barely registers (+20k). This is a
  register-shuffling structural gap, not a memory one — but it is the
  *bigger* part of the "overhead" (st+ld+mov+csr) story everywhere except
  blake3.

Share of the total instruction gap that is overhead classes at all:
blake3 20%, pulldown 69%, compression 18%, html 31%. The rest of each gap
is core-instruction differences (unrolling, inlining, addressing).

## 2. Where the stack excess concentrates

Top executed st+ld deltas per program (`dyn-spilldig.json`):

- **blake3**: `portable::compress_in_place` **+875k ld, -206k st**,
  mov -669k, csr +154k — the whole story. `compress_chunks_parallel`
  +106k st+ld, `compress_parents_parallel` +30k (driver-level pressure).
- **pulldown**: `FirstPass::parse_block` **+968k st+ld** (st +507k, ld +460k);
  `HtmlWriter` instantiation +487k; `handle_inline_pass1` +200k;
  `scalar_iterate_special_bytes` +158k (+110k csr).
- **compression**: `BrotliCompareAndPushToQueue` +56k (its 2,848-byte explicit
  histogram-array slot); `hq::UpdateNodes` +12k (spill-ld); nothing else
  matters — the program's +941k mov:edge is spread over the brotli-enc
  backward-reference/histogram functions (`BrotliCreateHqZopfli…` +359k).
- **html**: `Token::into_bytes` **+149k st** (+156k st+ld); the selectors-VM
  `Stack` `reserve`/grow path +155k; `TagScanner` actions +139k;
  `BytesCow::as_lowercase_string` +92k; dispatcher +69k; hashbrown `RawTable`
  growth +58k st; `decode_without_bom_handling` +43k.

Static frame census (`; frame:` remarks classified out/slot/spill/csr)
agrees: html's hot stack functions are **slot-dominated** — `into_bytes`
frame (0,344,0): 159 slot-st / 42 slot-ld vs 0 spill; `TagScanner` 40/23
slot vs 7/9 spill; `decode_without_bom_handling` 97 slot-st vs 20.
`parse_block` is mixed: 328 B slots (slot-st 213 / slot-ld 128) + 512 B
spills (spill-st 95 / spill-ld 182). `UpdateNodes` is genuine spill
traffic (spill-ld 112).

## 3. Mechanism ranking

### M1. x18 is allocatable on Linux but missing from the call clobber set — live miscompile, blocks a free register

`PLIRON_X18=1` exists opt-in (aarch64 Linux only:
`inst::regs::x18_allocatable`, `vendor/cranelift-codegen/src/isa/aarch64/inst/regs.rs:182`;
Linux default flag `set_x18_linux_default` at
`vendor/.../aarch64/mod.rs:277`). `get_machine_env_calls`
(`vendor/.../aarch64/abi.rs:1390,1402-1404`) adds x18 to the preferred set,
and `is_reg_saved_in_prologue` (:1610) correctly treats it as caller-saved —
**but `default_aapcs_clobbers` (:1657) covers only x0–x17**, and
`get_regs_clobbered_by_call` (:1420) patches in x30 when allocatable
(:1422-1425) and **never x18**. `clobbers_by_conv` (:1865-1881) routes every
normal `bl` — SystemV at :1876, the `(_, false)` catch-all at :1881 — to
`DEFAULT_AAPCS_CLOBBERS`. So a value held in x18 across a `bl` is modelled
as preserved while the callee (which does see x18 as allocatable, or any
external code) clobbers it.

Reproduced at HEAD: `harness/rastat.py dyn --only blake3-scalar
--env PLIRON_X18=1` builds a binary that **segfaults** (rc 139) mid-run.
Valgrind: invalid read in `words_from_le_bytes_64` inside
`compress_in_place`, then runaway `compress_subtree_wide → join` recursion
→ stack overflow — the exact signature of the 2026-11-18 crash that made
the knob opt-in (`docs/history/2026-11-18-ra-five-start.md`). The crash was
never re-investigated; this is plausibly its root cause, and the fix is a
3-line mirror of the x30 pattern in `get_regs_clobbered_by_call`.

Why it matters for this report: LLVM's `compress_in_place` uses **all of
w16, w17, w18** (27, 27, 33 static uses) and ~54 stack insns; ours uses
w17+x30 (x17 default-on, X30=3), no w16/w18, and 73 stack insns
(52 ldur+6 ldp loads vs LLVM 29 ldr+8 ldp). Under `clif-check`
(`CLIF_CHECK_TRIPLE=aarch64-unknown-linux-musl`), `PLIRON_X18=1` alone
takes `compress_in_place` 905→897 insts, 66→61 stack accesses — the earlier
dynamic estimate was ~-400k executed. The ld gap there is +875k, so x18 is
worth roughly half of it.

Fix (for the vendor owner): in `get_regs_clobbered_by_call` add, next to the
x30 block,
```rust
if x18_allocatable() {
    set.add(xreg_preg(18));
}
```
then flip `PLIRON_X18` default-on (Linux path already gates the triple) and
re-run the held-out A/B that crashed on 11-18.

### M2. x16 — the other register LLVM uses that we cannot

LLVM also allocates x16 (IP0): same clobber-by-call treatment as x17, which
we already do for x17. Ours is `spilltmp_reg()` (`inst/regs.rs:122`) — the
emit-time scratch for far accesses/veneers/atomics — so making it
allocatable needs an alternate post-regalloc scratch strategy (LLVM solves
the same problem; its emergency paths pick a different scratch when x16 is
live). Medium-hard; second ~half of the register-starvation story on
blake3 together with x18.

### M3. Explicit-slot traffic (IR-level temps, not regalloc spills)

The dominant html class. `into_bytes` copies a 144-byte `Token` onto the
stack and serializes from the temp (+149k executed stores, the program's
single largest st delta). `BrotliCompareAndPushToQueue` keeps a 2,848-byte
histogram array in a slot. `parse_block` is ~half slot / half spill.
None of this is reachable by the allocator: the temp disappears only if a
mid-end pass promotes it (SROA/`PLIRON_SROA_*` family), forwards through it
(memcpyopt; the open `PLIRON_MEMCPYOPT_SRCFWD` item notes
`exec_without_attrs`' 144-B copy blocked on a `RawVec::grow_one` nocapture),
or the sret destination is threaded through. Fixable mechanism, active
workstreams, biggest lever for html (+651k st+ld is mostly this).

### M4. Reload-per-use spill placement / minimal bundles (register starvation × split granularity)

The blake3 asymmetry is diagnostic: st **-206k** but ld **+875k** — we store
*less* than LLVM and load *more*. Our allocator spills 11 of the 16 message
words as minimal bundles → one `ldur` per use; LLVM reloads a spilled word
once into a register and covers several uses. So the same spill decisions
cost ~2-3× the loads. The allocator's own census says the same thing
statically: `xblock` is 40% of stack-side move weight on the std corpus —
a value in a register in block A and on the stack in block B pays an edge
spill/reload. Two ways at it: relieve pressure (M1+M2 do — this is why they
rank first: one more register converts whole bundles from stack to register,
which is strictly better than better reload placement), or widen minimal
bundles to cover multiple uses when a register is briefly free
(RELOADPRE/RELOADCSE are already on and evidently don't catch this shape).

### M5. `mov:edge` — blockparam/phi copies (biggest class on 3 of 4 programs)

+1.12M html, +941k compression, +572k pulldown. Top: brotli
`BrotliCreateHqZopfliBackwardReferences` +359k, html `TagScanner` +185k,
pulldown `parse_block` +247k. These are regalloc2's block-parameter edge
copies — the parallel-copy problem plus how many blockparams our pipeline
keeps alive vs LLVM's phi structure. The `bp-ovl-mix` census kind (23.6% of
std stack-side weight, and the html reg-reg heavyweight) is the same
phenomenon. Mechanism owned elsewhere (edge-move structural work); listed
here because it, not stack traffic, is the largest fixable-looking overhead
class on pulldown/compression/html.

### M6. Cold-call CSR splitting — already exists, already measured flat

`PLIRON_RA2_COLDSPLIT` (opt-in,
`docs/history/2026-11-13-six-features.md` §5): held-out csr saves
-0.1..-5.1% but stack traffic up to +5% and instructions 0.993–1.007.
The remaining csr gaps are hot-path pressure, not cold calls:
blake3's +154k csr is `compress_in_place`'s own prologue; html's largest
single csr source is `utf8_valid_up_to` (+171k) which is the
shape problem in §4, not cold-call liveness. Keep opt-in; a PGO-fed revisit
is a stretch item, not a mechanism for this excess.

## 4. The `check_str_for_latin1_and_bidi` anomaly — verdict

Corpus census (html, `PLIRON_RA2_MOVESTATS=1`): `bp-ovl-mix` reg-reg moves
are 33.0k weighted of which **30.92k is this one function** — the "42.5k of
49.6k" line in the earlier notes. The vcode (`/tmp/latin1.vcode`) is a
~180-block unrolled byte-scan: every early-exit edge carries the scan index
as a blockparam move. Verdict: **input-shape artifact**, not an allocator
defect — static corpus weighting prices each cold exit edge like a hot
block; its executed share is negligible. No standalone symbol survives in
either binary (inlined everywhere in both).

Its *binary-level* shadow is real though: `utf8_valid_up_to` on html runs
**+503k insts** vs LLVM (1.54M vs 1.04M) with +171k csr and +64k frame —
ours is 665 static insns vs LLVM's 219 and saves ~8 callee-saved regs at
each of ~21.4k calls; LLVM's version executes **zero** stack or csr
instructions over the whole run. Same source function (`encoding_rs`),
different shape: ours carries the multi-byte/latin1-check state machine
inline with 7–8 long-lived values, LLVM's stays caller-saved-register-only.
Fixable axis is inlining/outlining shape parity, not the allocator.

## 5. Residual notes

- `mov:edge` counts include the parallel-copy moves regalloc2 must emit;
  LLVM pays the same class at ~1/4–1/24 the rate. Some of our excess is
  likely unavoidable given blockparam-heavy lowering (`tailmergep`
  forwarders, `coldedge` adapters add edges), but x3.9–x24 is structural,
  not a tax.
- blake3 non-overhead remainder (~+1.46M of the +1.82M gap) is core
  arithmetic/unrolling inside `compress_in_place` — out of scope here.
- Data: `target/rastat/dyn-spilldig.json` (totals + per-function classes);
  corpora `target/corpus/{html,blake3,pulldown,std}`;
  `PLIRON_X18` build at `target/heldout/ir-pliron-x18/` (segfaulting —
  delete with other `ir-pliron-*` dirs, ~0.5 GB each);
  partial callgrind `target/heldout/ir-out/blake3-scalar.pliron-x18.instr.callgrind`
  (44.5M insts to the crash).

## 6. Follow-up: the drop-glue inline lever — measured dead end

The dig named `pop_up_to`'s escape-copy drop temps as html's largest
stack-store cluster (+124k st of the +651k). The obvious fix — inline the
small drop glue LLVM keeps inlining — was driven through every narrowing
of `PLIRON_INLINE_GLUET` and measured (`ab.py`, 15 rounds; `placement.py`
callgrind where noted):

| side | html instr | compression instr |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `GLUET=90` (blanket) | +0.2% | **+2.1%** (846 newly inlined glue sites) |
| `GLUET=90` + floor only when a ptr arg is (gep of) an alloca | +0.3% | **+2.2%** (251 sites; compression's drops are locals too) |
| `GLUET=90` + leaf glue only (`ncalls<=1`, LLVM's `__rust_dealloc` shape) | — | **+2.1%** (+1.18M exact: `ProcessCommandsInternal` +649k, `BrotliCreateHqZopfliBackwardReferences` +532k) |
| `GLUET=300 DUPCOST=0 GROWTH=100000` (all glue inlined) | **+0.8%** | — |

Key facts:

- LLVM's `ProcessCommandsInternal` keeps `drop_glue<HuffmanTreeGroup>` a
  call at 6 sites and calls `__rust_dealloc` directly 9x; our floor
  variants paste leaf/multi-call glue into its hot loop and self time
  goes 116→574 samples. **Partial glue inlining is strictly worse than
  none**: a temp escaping through any surviving call still pins, so the
  pasted body only adds traffic.
- LLVM's `pop_up_to` is 356 insns, 8 direct `__rust_dealloc`, **zero**
  glue calls, st 44 / ld 87. Forced-full-inlining ours reaches 1144 insns,
  7 dealloc, st 50 / ld 216 — the calls went away and the stack traffic
  stayed. `PLIRON_SROA_WHYFN=pop_up_to` under the forced build: the
  64–144B `escape copy` refusals are gone but the temps now refuse
  `accesses: out of range / self-copy` (14×24B, 4×32B, 2×144B) — the
  sret→`llvm.memcpy`→drop chain pins them (v15903 `pop` sret temp →
  v16101 144B temp → field drops; the temp's address also flows through
  `br` block args = `flow`).
- Verdict: html's drop-temp stack excess is not a calls problem. LLVM
  dissolves the whole move chain end-to-end (sret promote + SROA +
  glue inlining iterate until the object is SSA); our temps keep memory
  shape through memcpy and phi-flow patterns sroa refuses.
- Open axes if revisited: (a) `memcpyopt::run_srcfwd` currently forwards
  temp→param reads incl. consuming-drop args (memcpyopt.rs:3217) — a
  temp→alloca generalization would forward the drop args to the sret
  source temp and kill the memcpy + dst temp outright; (b) sroa's
  `out of range / self-copy` and `flow` refusal classes.
- `PLIRON_INLINE_GLUET` stays opt-in; the alloca/leaf gate experiments
  were reverted (in-tree comment at inline.rs:1603 records the numbers).
  PARITY.md codegen item 12 updated to "wrong lever" with pointers.
