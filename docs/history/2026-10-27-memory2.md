# Compile memory, part 2: `LOWERFREE`, `JEARENA`, `CGU_ACAP`

Follows docs/history/2026-10-26-memory.md. All three flags are default off
and change no output byte (docs/determinism.md R3).

## 1. Where the peak was

`PLIRON_MEM_MARK=1` timeline of syn at `-O` (16 CGUs, DEADFREE on):

- Every CGU starts stage A within ~0.5 s of its submit (there are more
  workers than CGUs), so all 16 inline at once.
- Stage B waits for the facts barrier. syn's biggest CGU, cgu.01 (ord 2),
  publishes last, at about 1.3 s, so ords 3–7 and then 8–15 hold their
  post-inline IR for up to a second doing nothing.
- After stage B lowers a CGU, the CGU keeps its whole pliron `Context` until
  the object is written: phase (A) lowers on one thread, then phase (B)
  drains the shared fn pool for 0.3–2.4 s per CGU, with every CGU queued at
  once. The finished `FnJob`s also keep their CLIF bodies and lowering facts
  until phase (C).
- jemalloc: rustc's arenas keep freed pages dirty for 10 s and then muzzy
  for another 10 s, and each thread has its own arena. A process-wide
  `_RJEM_MALLOC_CONF=dirty_decay_ms:0,muzzy_decay_ms:0` brings syn from
  1,800 to 1,276 MiB. `dirty_decay_ms:100` or `:1000` does almost nothing
  (no background thread, so decay runs only on allocation ticks).
  `narenas:4` alone gives 1,555. `background_thread:true` took 45 s.
- At the end of the compile, syn's footprint is still 957 MiB (193 at the
  first submit). With both freeing flags it is 616 MiB. The "15 MB per CGU
  retained" in the earlier note is mostly dirty pages in thread arenas.

## 2. The flags

- **`PLIRON_LOWERFREE=1`** (native, no `-g`). `lower_module` serializes the
  data initializers right after phase (A), the last read of the `Context`.
  It then releases the `Context` and drains phase (B). `run_jobs` is now
  split into `start_jobs` and `finish_jobs` around that point.
  `lower_to_object` takes a `CtxSlot`, either `Ref(&Context)` or
  `Own(&mut Option<Context>)`; cgupool passes `Own`. A compiled `FnJob`
  `shed`s its CLIF body and fact tables. It keeps `func.params`, which
  relocations resolve through. Use after free can't compile: `ctx` is
  borrowed from the slot until the release. The only later reader,
  `-g`'s DWARF, goes through `CtxSlot::get` and panics if the `Context` has
  been released.
- **`PLIRON_JEARENA=1`** (`pliron-cfg/src/jearena.rs`). It finds rustc's
  `_rjem_mallctl` with `dlsym` and creates `PLIRON_JEARENA_N` (default 4)
  arenas with decay `PLIRON_JEARENA_DECAY` ms (default 0). It binds each
  CGU worker thread (`step_once`) and each fn-pool thread to one of them.
  rustc's own threads keep their arenas. Without jemalloc it does nothing.
  Binding the main thread too gains only 3% more (1,310 → 1,269 MiB), so
  it is left out.
- **`PLIRON_CGU_ACAP=N`**: at most N CGUs between the start of stage A and
  done. `PLIRON_CGU_INFLIGHT` stops rustc's main thread instead.
  Under `ACAP` the main thread keeps defining, so rustc's serial front half
  stays off the critical path; only the start of stage A (the inliner's
  growth) waits. A worker with no admissible job steals fn-pool work.
  Stage A starts in ordinal order, so the started set is closed downwards
  and every stage-B wait inside it can be met: no deadlock. The cap is off
  while two-phase facts hold.

## 3. Numbers

`lockf`-held, median of 3, pliron side of `ctime.py`'s commands.
`base` is the build before these changes. `F` is `LOWERFREE=1 JEARENA=1`.

| input | base | F | F + ACAP=8 | F + ACAP=6 | LLVM (10-26) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| kernels | 245 MiB 0.29 s | 190 0.28 | 190 0.28 | 190 0.29 | 149 |
| kernels-wasm | 260 0.61 | 213 0.64 | 213 0.61 | 212 0.61 | 129 |
| regex-syntax | 815 1.49 | 545 1.59 | 471 1.70 | 484 1.70 | 295 |
| regex-wasm | 766 2.77 | 666 2.85 | 667 2.75 | 668 2.78 | 254 |
| syn | 1,794 3.97 | 1,341 4.04 | 1,113 4.17 | 1,020 4.27 | 574 |

Repeating `base` at the end: syn 1,841 MiB / 3.96 s, regex-syntax 836 / 1.54.
The new build with no flags matches `base` (syn 1,795 / 3.89).

Single flags on syn and regex-syntax:

| | syn | regex-syntax |
|---|---|---|
| LOWERFREE | 1,733 3.71 | 771 1.53 |
| JEARENA | 1,460 3.77 | 622 1.56 |
| ACAP=8 | 1,518 3.96 | 733 1.62 |
| F + ACAP=4 | 933 4.87 | 461 1.79 |

Card arithmetic, geomean over the five inputs against `base`:

- **F**: memory ×0.77 (+1.9 overall), time +3.6% (−0.3).
- **F + ACAP=8**: memory ×0.72 (+2.3 overall), time +2.8% against `base`
  and +4.3% against the flagless new build (about −0.3 to −0.45).

The two freeing flags are nearly free. LOWERFREE and JEARENA cost nothing
alone; together they cost about 3% on syn, and regex-wasm's +3% is
JEARENA's purging. ACAP's time cost grows fast below 6.

## 4. Checks

- `test.sh lowerfree`: 8-CGU tests/std with LOWERFREE + JEARENA. Each of
  the per-CGU objects must be byte-identical to the flags-off build, the
  binary must match stock rustc, and the stats line must show the
  `Context` was released.
- `detcheck.py` adds the variants `memfree` (LOWERFREE + JEARENA) and
  `acap2`.
- The gate passes plain (76 jobs) and with `LOWERFREE=1 JEARENA=1
  CGU_ACAP=2` (76 jobs). With the same flags, fuzz seeds 11700–11999 pass
  300 of 300.
- `detcheck.py --variants again,memfree,acap2,inflight1` over regex-syntax,
  syn and the smoke programs: every object is byte-identical.

## Remaining levers, ranked

1. Flip LOWERFREE + JEARENA on after a crates/UI pass, and pick an ACAP
   value. It is per machine: 8 on this 14-core Mac. A cap relative to the
   core count, or to footprint, would carry over to other machines.
2. The barrier wait. The heaviest CGU (syn's cgu.01, regex-syntax's
   cgu.00) publishes last and holds back every higher ordinal. Splitting
   it, or ordering ordinals by size (which needs the facts merge to be
   independent of order), shortens the window in which every post-inline
   IR is resident.
3. `Operation` is 160 B. `Location` is about 48 B of that and is almost
   always unknown, so boxing it saves about 40 B per op: about 70 MB at
   syn's 1.7M ops.
4. LOWERFREE for wasm. `emit_obj` and `wsig` still read the `Context`
   after CLIF lowering. regex-wasm, at 2.6x LLVM, is now the worst ratio.
5. Fewer ops after inlining (2.7M against LLVM's 160k instructions), and
   constants as separate ops.
