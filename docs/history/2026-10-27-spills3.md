# Spills, part 3: by-value argument copies of a loop-carried local

Follow-up to 2026-10-27-spills2, whose top leftover was the parser
functions of regex-syntax (`parse_with_comments`, `pop_group`, `pop_class`:
200–320 spill accesses each). One default-off flag:

| flag | where | what |
|---|---|---|
| `PLIRON_BYVALHOME=1` | `pliron-ir-opt/src/memcpyopt.rs` (`try_byval`, `run_byval`), `sroa.rs` (`State::homes`), `src/cgupool.rs` (step `byvalhome`), `src/builder.rs` (`CallInfo::arg_byval_ro`) | a by-value argument copy of a local becomes the local itself, and the local stays one stack object |

## Cause

The author of spills2 saw it right: `concat = self.push_group(concat)?`
copies the 72-byte `Concat` into an argument temp (`memcpy(%t, %concat,
72); call push_group(%sret, %self, %t)`), the callee writes a
`Result<Concat>` through sret, and the payload is copied back into
`concat`. SROA splits `concat` into nine 8-byte slices and promotes them;
the copy into `%t` becomes nine stores, the copy back nine loads, and the
nine SSA values are live around the parser loop and across its calls —
spilled. LLVM's SROA leaves a partition that only memcpys touch in
memory, so there `concat` stays a stack object and the copies are `q`
register pairs.

Three attempts that did not work, for the record:

- Keeping copy-only allocas in memory (spills2's experiment) loses
  elsewhere: without forwarding, every link of a copy chain becomes a
  real copy.
- Treating the call operand as an SROA `Escape` (rustc's `captures(none)`
  on the by-value param) and leaving such locals unsplit: the parser's
  temps are not `Escape`s but `Pin`s with extent 72 — rustc does not
  deduce `captures(none)` for these params — so the rule never fired.
- Forwarding only when the callee's by-value param is `readonly` (rustc's
  `DeducedParamAttrs::read_only`, now recorded as
  `CallInfo::arg_byval_ro`): `push_group`, `pop_group` and
  `push_alternate` all mutate their `concat` before moving it on, so only
  3 copies in the crate qualified.

## Fix

`memcpyopt::try_byval`, run as its own step (`byvalhome`) right before
SROA — after `phisimp` (the inliner binds callee params through block
arguments, and a home's address must not flow through one) and after the
`nocapture` inference. For `memcpy(%t, %s, n); ...; call f(.., %t, ..)`:

- `%t` is a fresh `n`-byte alloca used only by this copy (as
  destination), lifetime markers, and one call operand whose
  `arg_extent` is exactly `n` (a by-value Indirect param).
- `%s` is a constant offset into an alloca `%a` (not promoted, aligned
  for `%t`), whose address never leaves the function's view
  (`home_users`: loads, store addresses, mem intrinsics, lifetime
  markers, GEPs, and call operands with a known extent that rustc or our
  inference marks non-captured; drop glue counts as neither a read nor a
  write — see below).
- No write to `%a[off, off+n)` between the copy and the call, and no
  other operand of the call reaches into `%a`.
- The callee may write its by-value copy, which is now `%a`'s bytes. So
  `dead_after`: on every path from the call (its unwind pad included)
  those bytes are fully overwritten, or `%a`'s lifetime ends, or the
  function leaves, before any read of them. A budgeted CFG walk
  (64 blocks, 4,096 ops) over `%a`'s own users — nothing else can reach
  its bytes. MIR guarantees the shape: the local was moved into the call,
  so nothing reads it before a new value is stored.
- The walk must also show the bytes *rewritten* on some path (the
  loop-carried shape), or the home must be at least 64 bytes. A small
  temp that is consumed and never rewritten promotes better: its scalars
  feed the copy directly, and 24-byte `Range`s passed to
  `RangeError::report` and `Hir::concat` grew `hir::interval` functions
  by 64–224 B each when they were homed.

Then the call takes `%s`, the copy and `%t` go, and `%a` is recorded in
`State::homes`: SROA's `split` leaves it alone (like `escape_copies`).
Split, every other access would scalarize and the by-value operand would
become a `Pin` (or `Escape`) copy of the scalars again — the copy this
removes. The copy back from the sret result stays a 72-byte memcpy
(lowered to scalar load/store pairs on a stack slot), field reads are
loads, and slotprom may still promote hot fields at the CLIF level with
`SLOTPROM_SYNCCOST` weighing the sync.

Drop glue (`core::ptr::drop_in_place`, `drop_glue<T>`, by symbol as
lowering already does) on the home is treated as touching nothing: after
the by-value call the local is moved out (its drop flag is false until a
new value is stored) or `Copy` (no drop glue), and a partially moved
struct drops its remaining fields through field addresses. Without this
the flag-guarded `drop_in_place(&concat)` in the call's unwind pad made
every candidate "read after the call". An inlined drop appears as plain
loads and still counts.

## Tests

- `tests/byval_home` (stanza `byvalhome`): a parser-shaped loop that moves
  a 72-byte aggregate through `?` calls into non-inlined callees that
  write their copy, reads it between the moves, rebuilds it from sret
  results, and drops it on the unwind path only when still initialized.
  Output must match with the flag off and on, at `-O` and `-O0` (and
  stock rustc); `parse`'s frame must shrink (416 → 192 B; 6 copies
  forwarded).
- Gate plain and with `PLIRON_BYVALHOME=1` plus the spills2 flags; fuzz
  seeds 14000–14300 with the same flags.

## Left

1. `pop_group`/`pop_class` are unchanged (368/408 B of spill slots): their
   traffic is the 160–224-byte `GroupState`/`ClassState` popped off the
   stack and taken apart field by field — copy-only chains SROA
   scalarizes, with no call to home them against. The next step is
   memcpy-chain forwarding (`memcpy(b, a); memcpy(c, b)` with `b` dead →
   `memcpy(c, a)`) so that a copy-only alloca can stay in memory without
   each link becoming a real copy.
2. Candidates this pass rejects on regex-syntax: 20 whose source is a
   loaded pointer (a by-value copy out of the heap), 12 whose call sits in
   another block, 13 read after the call. The by-value copies into
   `Vec::push`-style callees of 24-byte values are left to promotion on
   purpose.
3. One reload per `br_table` edge instead of one at the join (regalloc2),
   from spills2's list.
